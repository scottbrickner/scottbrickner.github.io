The through-line

One bloated module → ten units → a system

Microlearning isn’t a short lecture. It’s the discipline of deciding what’s separable, building each piece to one objective and one moment, and wiring the pieces into a library that keeps them fresh.

The layer that makes it stick

Retrieval that targets confident misses

Two items per unit; each item appears twice, not once (double exposure beat single in Price et al., 26,258 physicians). The highest-yield targeting rule: resurface what learners got wrong while confident. Try it — rate how sure you are, then answer.

Where microlearning stops

The honest half: some of this isn’t a microlearning job

Fragmenting integrated reasoning doesn’t reduce cognitive load — it removes the scaffolding that made it coherent, and load goes up.

Red light · belongs in simulation

“Is the AF the problem, or a symptom of the problem?”

Your post-op day-one patient is in AF with RVR and hypotensive. Is the rhythm causing the pressure, or the pressure causing the rhythm? Tamponade? Hypovolemia from chest-tube output? Pain? Hypoxia? Rate-control now, or call the surgeon?

many variablestime pressureincomplete infono single right answer

That’s integrated reasoning → simulation + debrief + coach. Microlearning carries the separable knowledge and keeps it fresh, so sim time is spent on the reasoning — not on teaching what magnesium has to do with anything.
